My plan and its purpose

This two-week plan supports my goals of walking regularly, building balanced meals, and developing a short stress-management habit. Walking will be my main activity, supported by simple home strength exercises and gentle stretching.

The meal plan uses familiar foods that can be prepared at home. My dietary goals are to include vegetables, protein, and carbohydrates in one main meal on at least five days each week, choose a variety of foods, and drink fluids regularly. I am focusing on consistent habits rather than a weight-loss target.

Fitness activities and intensity

Walking

I will complete five 20-minute walks each week at moderate intensity. This means breathing faster while still being able to talk, although singing would be difficult. A continuous university walk can count if it meets the planned duration and intensity. I will not count the same walk twice.

Walking preparation and recovery

Before each walk, I will walk slowly for three minutes. Afterwards, I will walk slowly for two minutes and gently stretch my calves and thighs for three minutes. Each walking session will therefore take approximately 28 minutes in total.

Home strength exercises

I will complete a 10–15-minute session on Tuesday and Friday each week. This includes two minutes of gentle marching and shoulder movements beforehand, the exercises and rest periods, and two minutes of gentle stretching afterwards. I will begin at a light-to-moderate effort while learning controlled movements.

Rest and recovery

Wednesday and Sunday will have no planned moderate exercise. I can continue normal daily movement and do optional gentle stretching.

Simple home strength exercises

I will use a stable chair placed against a wall and a clear floor space. I will move slowly, breathe normally, and rest for approximately 30–60 seconds between exercises.

Weekly frequency

Five walking sessions, two home strength sessions, five breathing sessions, and two recovery days.

Progression

My planned moderate walking totals 100 minutes per week. This is a starting routine rather than the full adult activity recommendation. WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week and muscle-strengthening activities involving the major muscle groups on two or more days. My short strength sessions introduce basic movements; I will gradually develop their difficulty and increase walking time as my consistency and confidence improve.

Timing

I will use a suitable university journey or walk in the early evening. In hot weather, I will choose a cooler time or an indoor location. On strength days, I can complete the exercises separately from the walk if that fits my schedule better.

Hydration

I will spread drinks across the day, using six to eight cups or glasses of fluid as a general guide. Water will be my main drink, while milk in the meal plan also contributes. I may need more fluid in hot weather or during longer periods of activity.

I will carry a refillable bottle to university and drink with meals and between classes. I will use thirst and pale-yellow urine as practical reminders to check whether I am drinking enough.

6–8 cups or glasses a day

Recording my progress

I will use a short daily phone note to record:

  • Walking duration and whether the planned session was completed.
  • Strength exercises and repetitions on Tuesday and Friday.
  • Whether one main meal included vegetables, protein, and carbohydrates.
  • Breathing-session completion and stress ratings before and afterwards.
  • Any difficulty or adjustment that affected the plan.

At the end of each week, I will compare my records with the targets of five walks, five balanced meals, and five breathing sessions. Strength sessions will be recorded separately as part of the fitness plan.

Reflection on my plan

I think this plan is realistic because it builds on walking and home-cooked food, which are already part of my life. The strength exercises are short and can be completed at home. However, scheduling and recording these activities will require more consistency than my current routine.

My main challenges may be university commitments, hot weather, and having limited control over meals prepared at home. I can respond by choosing suitable walking times and using similar foods when a planned meal is unavailable. I can also complete my walk and strength exercises at different times on the same day.

The plan could help me develop a clearer activity routine, learn basic strength movements, and pay more attention to food variety and hydration. I also want to find out whether a short breathing break is useful after studying. I will judge progress using my records rather than assume that I have improved. After two weeks, I will review what I completed, what was difficult, and which habits I can maintain.
